A crop changes what a photograph explains. Removing distractions can clarify the center of interest, but removing the decisive object or a necessary relationship can change the meaning. Choose the crop for the content rather than making the image obey the first box you drew.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the person, action and object that carry the photograph’s meaning.
Step 2. Remove unnecessary space while preserving that relationship.
Step 3. Check context, image direction and the final layout before committing the crop.

Worked example.
Name what must survive. Maya’s hands adjust the support beneath the bridge. The action depends on the hands, support and bridge being understood together. An empty edge may be expendable; the connection is not.
Compare a damaging crop. The crop keeps the face but removes the hands and support. The image now says less about the action. A tight crop is not automatically a stronger crop.
Protect the storytelling detail. A tighter view keeps the hands, support and relevant bridge structure. The crop removes distraction while retaining the reason the image matters. Recheck it in the actual page relationship.

Watch for this. Do not mistake closeness for clarity. The right crop is the one that preserves the relevant meaning.
