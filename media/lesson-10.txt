Visual hierarchy tells readers where to enter and what to read next. A dominant image should earn attention through its content and its size. The best anchor is a strong storytelling image, not simply the easiest photograph to enlarge.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the most informative image that supports the approved focus.
Step 2. Give it a clear size or placement advantage in the layout.
Step 3. Check the next reading steps: headline, related copy, supporting images and captions.

Worked example.
See equal-weight competition. Every photo is the same size; the headline and captions compete equally. Readers receive little guidance about which information matters most. Equal size is not always wrong, but here it hides the coverage focus.
Anchor the spread. The bridge test becomes dominant because it shows the central action. Its content and scale work together. A blurry or irrelevant photograph would not become a better anchor just by growing.
Guide the next move. Dominant action → headline and copy → related details and captions. This is a suggested reading path for the example, not a required template. Test whether your actual layout guides readers to the intended information.

Watch for this. A large decorative element can overpower the reporting without giving the reader a useful entry point.
