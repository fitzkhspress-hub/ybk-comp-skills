Composition directs attention through subject position, angle, lines, framing and separation from the background. These are tools for making the action understandable. They are not a checklist that makes a weak moment strong simply because a grid line appears.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the action you want the reader to notice.
Step 2. Change position or angle to clarify it without disrupting the event or compromising safe access.
Step 3. Inspect background distractions and the relationships within the frame.

Worked example.
See the competing background. A strong background line crosses the subject and attention drifts away from the hands. The frame contains the action, but its competing shapes make the relationship harder to see.
Choose a clearer angle. A slight change of viewpoint isolates the hands and support. The camera position helps the action separate from the background. Use approved access and safety procedures.
Use guides as tools. A thirds grid and leading line suggest ways to direct attention. Keep the composition because it explains the action, not because a subject mechanically touches a grid intersection.

Watch for this. Composition should not require staging a candid event and presenting it as unprompted coverage.
