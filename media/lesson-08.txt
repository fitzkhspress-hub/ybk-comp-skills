A headline is read as language before it is seen as a shape. Line breaks can interrupt a phrase or attach words to the wrong thought. Adjust the wording or arrangement so readers can follow the meaning while the text fits the approved design.

How to do it.
Step 1. Read the complete headline aloud for its grammatical meaning.
Step 2. Keep closely connected phrases together when choosing line breaks.
Step 3. Recheck the layout after edits and follow the staff’s capitalization and type conventions.

Worked example.
See the broken phrase. WEAK BREAK: “A shift in” / “support changes the test” The short first line strands “in” away from its object. The visual shape interrupts a connected phrase.
Preserve a language unit. CLEARER BREAK: “A shift in support” / “changes the test” The phrase stays together, making the relationship easier to read. Another accurate revision could also solve the fit.
Check the actual type. Fit the wording in the current headline style, then read it again. A line break that works in notes may change after type is sized or edited. Recheck the final layout rather than relying on an earlier draft.

Watch for this. Do not shrink a headline until it is unreadable just to preserve the first wording you tried.
