White space is active design space. It can separate unrelated packages, connect related elements or frame an important object. Proximity helps readers know which caption or copy belongs to which image; accidental gaps can suggest the wrong relationship.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the content that belongs together.
Step 2. Use spacing to show that grouping and distinguish unrelated material.
Step 3. Check for isolated pockets of unused space and ambiguous caption-to-photo connections.

Worked example.
Read the accidental relationship. A caption sits closer to the wrong photograph than to the image it explains. The reader may attach the words to the nearest picture. Accurate words can become misleading when the visual connection is ambiguous.
Group the related pieces. Bring the caption into an unmistakable relationship with its photograph. Adjacency or a clear labeling system can establish the connection. Apply the newsroom’s caption placement practices.
Give the remaining space a job. Space separates two packages and frames the dominant image. The gaps are intentional. Their purpose is not to make the page empty, but to make relationships easier to understand.

Watch for this. Filling every gap with decoration can make the grouping less clear rather than more complete.
