A portfolio shows choices as well as individual images. A useful selection combines strong storytelling and technical quality with distinct contributions: different moments, viewpoints or purposes. Repeating near-identical frames can hide the photographer’s range even when one frame is strong.

How to do it.
Step 1. Evaluate each candidate for meaning, technical usability and researched context.
Step 2. Compare what the images contribute as a set rather than selecting by isolated attractiveness.
Step 3. Replace redundant or weak choices and keep the identity and caption information needed to understand each frame.

Worked example.
See the repetition. Several similar views show almost the same moment and angle. A reader receives little new evidence of the photographer’s choices across the set.
Look for distinct contributions. A strong action moment, a revealing detail and a purposeful portrait contribute different information. These are examples of range, not a required category mix or portfolio size. Follow the actual assignment.
Proof the set and its context. Each selected image has a reason to remain and accurate supporting information. Do not keep a weak frame solely to create variety. The set should demonstrate both range and quality.

Watch for this. Range does not mean adding an unrelated or technically unusable picture just because it looks different.
