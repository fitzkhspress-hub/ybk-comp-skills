A spread is read across facing pages. Shared alignments and consistent spacing can make separate pieces feel related. A grid is a planning structure; use it to organize content rather than forcing photographs into shapes that damage their meaning.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the approved page grid, margins, gutter and spacing system.
Step 2. Align related edges and maintain the intended spacing between elements.
Step 3. Check the whole spread, including important content near the fold and safe areas specified by the production guide.

Worked example.
Spot arbitrary edges. Photo edges and text starts drift; spaces vary without a purpose. The mismatched edges make the layout harder to scan because the reader cannot recognize a consistent organizing system.
Create shared anchors. Related images and copy align to common guides across the spread. Alignment creates relationships. Follow the grid and spacing values supplied by your staff rather than taking dimensions from this diagram.
Review the facing pages. Look across the whole spread before checking each page separately. A composition can look balanced one page at a time and still conflict across the fold. Protect meaningful details using local production requirements.

Watch for this. Do not crop away a face, tool or decisive action merely because the first layout box was convenient.
