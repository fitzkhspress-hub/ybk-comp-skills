A people spread can help readers find identities while also revealing experiences. Consistent portrait organization supports recognition. Related secondary coverage should add a specific reported perspective without making the page harder to navigate.

How to do it.
Step 1. Verify portrait identities and the approved ordering system.
Step 2. Choose a secondary angle that contributes a relevant experience.
Step 3. Separate roles clearly so portrait labels remain easy to connect and the secondary package is coherent.

Worked example.
Protect the identification job. Portraits and names follow a consistent approved order. The order needs a verified roster. Visual similarity or memory is not enough to confirm that every label matches the right person.
Add a reported experience. A secondary package follows how students revised one failed design. This adds an experience rather than merely repeating the portrait names. Its facts and voices require reporting.
Keep both reading tasks clear. Portrait identities stay readable while the related package has its own entry point. The layout lets readers identify people and explore coverage without confusing which words belong to which image.

Watch for this. Do not sacrifice correct identity or readable labels to squeeze in an unrelated module.
