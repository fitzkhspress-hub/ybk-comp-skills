Repetition can connect a spread or a book through consistent visual choices. Spot color works best when it has an information job, such as linking related elements or marking a recurring role. More colors do not automatically create more meaning.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the staff’s approved palette and the content relationship you want to show.
Step 2. Repeat a treatment consistently where that relationship occurs.
Step 3. Remove accents or ornaments that compete with the reporting or suggest a false grouping.

Worked example.
Find the competing accents. Different bright accents mark unrelated objects with no consistent role. The viewer cannot tell whether the colors mean a section, a topic, an instruction or nothing.
Assign a clear role. One approved accent identifies related secondary coverage. Now the repetition communicates a relationship. The choice should come from the current staff system rather than an invented universal palette.
Remove purposeless treatment. The content stays prominent after unrelated ornaments are removed. A treatment earns its space by helping readers interpret, navigate or connect the coverage.

Watch for this. Repeating an ornament does not make it meaningful if it never serves the content.
