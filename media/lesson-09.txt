Coverage is a record of people’s experiences, not just a list of events. A reporting plan can reveal which actions, perspectives and people are missing. Additional voices should serve the approved focus and accurate representation rather than a quota invented by this lesson.

How to do it.
Step 1. Map the approved focus to needed actions, perspectives and facts.
Step 2. Compare the plan with the people already visible or quoted.
Step 3. Find a relevant missing experience and report it accurately, following local access and approval procedures.

Worked example.
Notice the narrow record. A draft spread shows the same builder in every image and quotation. The design may repeat one perspective while leaving another relevant role unexplained.
Find a meaningful gap. The recorder tracked each test, but the reporting never explains what the measurements showed. This is a reason to interview that person: their work adds information to the focus, not just another name.
Assign a reporting purpose. Ask the recorder how a measurement changed the group’s next decision. The added voice should connect to the coverage. Student access, approvals and naming practices remain governed by the newsroom.

Watch for this. Do not assume that adding a name automatically adds a useful perspective.
