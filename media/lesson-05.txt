Concise writing gives each word a job. Editing should reveal the actor and action, remove empty repetition and preserve the facts. A shorter sentence is not better if it deletes a needed qualification or changes the chronology.

How to do it.
Step 1. Find the actor and the concrete action.
Step 2. Replace inflated wording with direct language while keeping names, values and conditions accurate.
Step 3. Check the revision against source notes and apply the current staff style consistently.

Worked example.
Spot the buried actor. “A second test was conducted by Maya after the movement of the support.” The actor arrives late, and a noun phrase hides the action. The wording is longer than the information requires.
Make the actions visible. “Maya moved the support and tested the bridge again.” The revision names the person and orders the verified actions. It does not add an outcome that the source notes lack.
Protect the qualification. “The bridge held 18 washers in the second trial” does not mean it always held 18. Keep limits and timing that determine a fact’s meaning. Follow staff choices for tense, attribution, capitalization and second reference.

Watch for this. Do not delete a limitation merely because it makes the sentence shorter.
