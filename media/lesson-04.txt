Transitions and quotations do different jobs. A transition supplies the facts a reader needs to understand the next voice. A quotation adds the source’s perspective. Their relationship should move the story forward instead of repeating the same point twice.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify what the next quotation contributes.
Step 2. Supply the verified context needed to understand it.
Step 3. Remove a transition that merely paraphrases the quotation or inserts the writer’s opinion.

Worked example.
Identify the contribution. QUOTE: “I watched where it bent instead of guessing.” The source explains a change in approach. Readers need to know what prompted that change.
Add the missing context. TRANSITION: After the first bridge collapsed, Maya moved a support toward its center. This fact connects the earlier event to the following explanation. It does not tell readers how to feel.
Read the pair together. Verified change in the transition → source explains the reasoning. The pieces add different information. Use the staff’s current attribution, paragraph and tense conventions when preparing actual copy.

Watch for this. Do not write “She was determined to succeed” unless reporting establishes that interpretation; let the source explain her thinking.
