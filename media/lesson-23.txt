An ad or tribute has a communication purpose and verified content. Hierarchy should help readers understand the message, identify the person or organization and find any approved action or contact detail. Accuracy and readability remain essential even when the material is promotional.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the approved message and the information readers need.
Step 2. Verify names, dates, images and any contact or action details through the staff’s process.
Step 3. Arrange the content by importance and proof the actual layout and image relationships.

Worked example.
Name the communication job. PRACTICE MESSAGE: recognize a student’s contribution with an approved portrait and verified name. The purpose guides the hierarchy. The lesson does not authorize an advertisement, endorsement or purchase.
Remove competing emphasis. Many equal-size statements compete, while the verified name is difficult to find. A busy design can obscure the very person or message the ad is meant to communicate.
Proof the content in place. The key message leads; the name and approved details remain readable and accurate. Use local approval, advertising, image-use and business procedures before actual publication.

Watch for this. A professional-looking layout can still publish the wrong name, date or contact detail.
