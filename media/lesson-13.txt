Typography creates roles: a headline introduces, body text carries a story and a caption explains a photograph. Readers need readable size, contrast and spacing. A consistent staff type system helps establish those roles without decorative competition.

How to do it.
Step 1. Use the approved type styles to distinguish the jobs of headline, copy and caption.
Step 2. Check readability at the intended viewing or print size, especially over an image.
Step 3. Remove treatments that compete with the meaning and apply the system consistently.

Worked example.
Identify the readability problem. Low-contrast small text crosses a busy image. The words exist, but readers have to fight the background. The problem is a relationship among size, contrast, placement and detail.
Repair the relationship. Place readable text where its contrast and space support the content. Moving the text, using the approved contrast treatment or changing the crop can help. Keep important action unobscured.
Repeat the role, not decoration. Headlines, copy and captions follow their assigned staff styles. Consistency lets readers recognize the information job. Use local typeface choices rather than adopting a new font-count rule from this lesson.

Watch for this. A type treatment is not successful merely because it looks unusual. It needs to communicate clearly.
