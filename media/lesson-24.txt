A campaign gives an audience a clear, accurate message across different formats. Consistency means preserving the essential facts and intended action while adapting the layout to each format. A repeated design is not enough if the date, destination or purpose changes accidentally.

How to do it.
Step 1. Identify the approved audience, message and action.
Step 2. Verify the essential facts and preserve them across formats.
Step 3. Adapt size and hierarchy for each format, then test the actual destination or instruction.

Worked example.
Create the source of truth. APPROVED PRACTICE FACTS: yearbook pickup, Friday, library desk. The facts are fictional training material. Actual announcements or sales messages need current approval and verification.
Compare two formats. Poster: Friday, library. Social card: Thursday, front office. The formats look related but disagree about the action. Design consistency cannot repair conflicting instructions.
Adapt without changing facts. Both formats say Friday, library desk; each makes the action easy to find. Check any real link or destination through the current workflow. The lesson does not authorize sending or publishing a campaign.

Watch for this. Do not use invented urgency, a false availability claim or an unverified deadline to make a message more persuasive.
