A purposeful portrait presents a person with intent. An environment, object, expression or pose can communicate something relevant about them, but those choices need reporting and collaboration. Visual props should not invent an identity or imply a claim the person does not support.

How to do it.
Step 1. Learn something specific and accurate about the person.
Step 2. Choose a setting and composition that make that information visible.
Step 3. Check expression, background, light and technical quality while following local portrait and permission practices.

Worked example.
Start with the person. REPORTED INTEREST: Maya designs and tests cardboard structures. This gives a reason to consider a workspace or an actual project. It does not justify unrelated symbolic props.
Connect environment to meaning. A portrait includes Maya and her actual design materials. The setting contributes information about the person. A simpler background can also work if expression and composition serve the purpose.
Remove misleading competition. The relevant materials remain, while unrelated background distractions are reduced. The portrait should direct attention to the person and supported context, not turn the person into an invented character.

Watch for this. Do not present a directed portrait as a candid moment. Use the format your approved assignment calls for.
